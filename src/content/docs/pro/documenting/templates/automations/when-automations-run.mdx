## Automations are checked on completion, never on save

Tallyfy checks automations at set moments: when the process launches, when a task is completed
or reopened, and when the kick-off form is completed after launch. Typing in a form field and
saving it never starts an automation by itself.

What happens next depends on the kind of action.

- **[Deadline](/products/pro/documenting/templates/automations/actions/deadline-actions/) and [show/hide](/products/pro/documenting/templates/automations/actions/visibility-actions/) actions follow your answers.** Each time Tallyfy checks an automation that already ran, it works these out again if a changed answer changes whether one of its conditions is met, or the date a deadline counts from has moved. If neither changed, nothing moves.
- **[Reopen](/products/pro/documenting/templates/automations/actions/status-actions/), [assignment](/products/pro/documenting/templates/automations/actions/asignment-actions/) and webhook actions run at most once per process.** Once one of these has fired, it won't fire again in that process. Nobody gets the same assignment twice, and an outside system doesn't get the same webhook twice.

An automation can mix both kinds. Its deadline and show/hide actions can run again, and its
other actions still run only once.

Launch the same template again and you get a fresh process, where every automation starts from
scratch.

## The moments Tallyfy checks

**When the process launches.** Tallyfy looks at every automation with at least one condition on
a kick-off form field, because the kick-off answers are the only answers that exist yet.

**When a task is [completed or reopened](/products/pro/tracking-and-tasks/tasks/how-can-i-complete-or-reopen-tasks-in-tallyfy/).** Tallyfy looks at the automations whose conditions
involve that task or the form fields on it. This is why an automation reading a step's form field
doesn't fire the moment somebody types in the field. It fires when the task carrying that field
is completed.

**When the kick-off form is completed after launch.** Usually that's when someone re-opens the
kick-off form on a running process, changes an answer, and clicks **Complete**. Tallyfy checks
every automation with a kick-off condition again at that moment. Re-opening the form on its own
checks nothing. The steps are in [changing kick-off answers after
launch](/products/pro/launching/triggers/kick-off-forms/).

## When a date or an answer changes mid-process

Here's what happens when a date moves after its automation has run:

1. You enter a start date of 5 September and complete the task.
2. The automation fires and sets a dependent task's deadline to 6 September.
3. You reopen the task, change the start date to 15 September, and complete it again.
4. Tallyfy checks the automation again, sees the start date has moved, and moves the dependent task to 16 September.

Saving the new date without completing the task moves nothing. The check happens on completion.

Show and hide work the same way. Say a kick-off answer of "No" hid five steps at launch. Someone
re-opens the kick-off form, changes the answer to "Yes" and completes it. The automation that hid
those steps no longer matches, so Tallyfy shows them again. It works in the other direction too:
a step an automation showed is hidden again once that automation stops matching.

## What a re-check leaves alone

When Tallyfy checks an automation again after an answer changes, it leaves these alone.

- **Work that's under way.** Undoing a Show never hides a step whose work has started. A Hide leaves started steps alone too when the kick-off form is completed after launch, or a step is completed again or reopened, even from an automation that's matching for the first time. Only launch and a step's very first completion still hide a step that's in progress. A step counts as started when it's in progress or completed, has a saved form answer, or an assignee or guest has finished their part. The process's activity feed notes each step it kept. If that step isn't needed any more, [hide it by hand](/products/pro/tracking-and-tasks/tasks/manually-show-hide-tasks/).
- **A show or hide that still applies.** While an automation still matches, Tallyfy doesn't apply its show or hide again, even one that found the step already that way. So if someone hid or showed that step by hand since, their change stays. An automation that starts to match, for the first time or again after it stopped, does apply its show or hide, and so does one that checks whether a step was reopened (see below).
- **A step another automation still shows or hides.** If an automation stops matching while another automation that still matches applies the same show or hide to that step, the step stays as it is. The activity feed says it was kept because another automation still applies it. That other automation takes it over, and undoes it once it stops matching.
- **A show or hide that changed nothing.** If the step was already the way the automation asked for, there's nothing to undo. Tallyfy drops it without touching the step.
- **Automations that check whether a step was reopened.** What they show or hide is never undone, so a step shown for the rework stays after the reopened step is completed again. They keep no record of what they applied, so while one matches it applies its show or hide again on each re-check, even over a change someone made by hand.
- **Deadlines someone set by hand.** Once a person changes a date on a running task, automations and the step chain don't move it. The activity feed says the automation kept it because its deadline was set by hand. See [set an exact date on a task](/products/pro/tracking-and-tasks/tasks/edit-task/change-a-task-due-date/), and the limit in the next section.
- **A deadline that counts from itself.** A deadline action applies once only when it shifts a step's own due date, like "3 days later than its current deadline". The same goes for one that counts from its own step, or from a step whose date counts from it through the step chain or another automation. Applying it again would move the date it counts from too, so it would never stop.
- **A deadline whose automation stops matching.** Only show and hide are undone. A deadline stays where the automation last put it.
- **A deadline that works out to the same date.** Nothing moves, and nothing is reported as changed.

## Processes that were already running

In a process that was already running when Tallyfy started re-applying automations, the first
time Tallyfy checks an automation again, it notes where the answers stand and moves nothing.
Answers changed after that are followed as this page describes.

For the kick-off form, that first check is the first **Complete** after that, so a change
made then isn't followed. Before you change an answer on such a process, click **Re-open** and
**Complete** once without changing anything, then **Re-open** the form again to make the change.
If you've already changed one, **Re-open** the form, put the old answer back and click
**Complete**, then **Re-open** it, make the change again and click **Complete**. Setting the date
by hand isn't a fix, because a date set by hand doesn't follow an automation again unless it's
[reset to automatic through the API](/products/pro/integrations/open-api/code-samples/tasks/reset-task-deadline/). A step doesn't
need this: reopening it notes the old answer first, so the change made after that is followed.

Dates typed by hand before Tallyfy started marking them aren't marked as set by hand after the
fact. Automations and the step chain can still move those dates.

## A deadline an automation moves pushes the steps behind it

A step whose deadline counts from another step follows it. When an automation moves the other
step's deadline, every unfinished step counting from it is worked out again. That's the same thing
that happens when someone completes that step or edits its date by hand.

The one exception is a step whose own date somebody set by hand. It keeps that date, and the
steps behind it count from it.

Nobody is named as having made an automation's change. Tallyfy posts no comment in the name of
the person whose completion ran the automation, sends no notification in their name, and doesn't
email the task's watchers. When an automation moves a date after launch, the task it moved gets a
comment that names nobody, saying its deadline moved on its own and that nobody edited it. Each
step behind it whose date moved gets one too, saying which step it follows. Neither comment is
posted at launch itself, and a hidden step gets neither. A step that was hidden when the check
started gets no "moved on its own" comment, even if the same automation shows it in that check. A step whose new date
was moved onto a working day also gets a note saying so.

## Emails when a step appears

After launch, the first time an automation shows a step, the step's assignees get the ["It's your
turn" email](/products/pro/integrations/email/emails-tallyfy-sends/task-activity/). A show applied
again on a later check sends none, and neither does a step that comes back because a hide was
undone.

## Other reasons an automation does nothing

Running isn't the same as having an effect. An automation can fire and still change nothing.

- **The target task is already completed.** Deadline, visibility and assignment actions all skip a task that's finished. There's nothing to shift or hide.
- **A condition reads a field that's still empty.** An empty field is a real value to Tallyfy and it gets compared like any other. A deadline action pointed at an empty date field does nothing, and the task keeps the deadline it already had.
- **The organization's plan doesn't run automations.** Automations run on Pro, Enterprise and AppSumo plans, and during a free trial. A Docs plan doesn't run them, and neither does an organization whose trial has ended without a paid plan. They're still saved on the template, and they start working as soon as the organization is on a plan that runs them.

## Order within one process

Several automations can fire from the same task completion. One automation can also carry several
actions. Where two of them touch the same step, the result depends on which one Tallyfy gets to
last, and that isn't something you should design around.

If a step's deadline has to follow another step's deadline, set that up in the step's
[Timings row](/products/pro/documenting/templates/edit-templates/step-timings/) rather than with
two automations that race each other.
