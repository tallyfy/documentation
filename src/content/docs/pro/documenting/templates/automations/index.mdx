## Why Tallyfy is the AI control layer

| What Tallyfy does | Why it matters in the age of AI |
|---|---|
| Define process steps | You can't automate anything without a recipe. |
| Hand small tasks to AI | Massive reduction in mistakes, omissions, and hallucination. |
| People approve work | Accountability. You can't blame AI for mistakes. |
| Hybrid people + AI tasks | AI can't do every task in a process. |
| Track real-time status | Tracking AI sessions at scale is a nightmare. |
| Gradually shift tasks to AI | A total re-do will break a process that works today. |

## What are automations?

Each automation you add to a [template](/products/pro/documenting/templates/) has an If side and a Then side. They make each [process](/products/pro/tracking-and-tasks/processes/) adapt based on what users enter in [form fields](/products/pro/tracking-and-tasks/tasks/what-are-form-fields-in-tallyfy/) and what happens to [tasks](/products/pro/tracking-and-tasks/tasks/). For AI-powered task completion, see [BYO AI integration](/products/pro/integrations/byo-ai/).

Automations are also what keep a process reliable when AI is involved, since each task gets checked and routed before the next one runs. See why that matters in the [AI task reliability calculator](https://tallyfy.com/tools/ai-task-reliability/).

:::tip[One template can handle several cases]
Say a support [template](/products/pro/documenting/templates/) asks for the ticket type. One automation can assign "Complaint" tickets to a specialized team, and another can show a step that adds a "Feature Request" to the product backlog. Each [process](/products/pro/tracking-and-tasks/processes/) then follows the path its own answers pick.
:::

### What an automation is made of

Every automation has two parts: conditions and actions.

#### Conditions (the "IF" part)

Conditions tell the automation *when* to fire. Two categories:

**Step conditions** - based on what happens to a task:
- Task completed, reopened, approved, rejected, acknowledged, or expired
- Task not assigned

**Form field conditions** - based on data entered in step form fields or kick-off form fields:
- **contains** / **does not contain** a value
- **is** / **is not** a value (text fields don't offer these)
- **is any of** several values
- **is greater than** / **is less than** (for numbers)
- **is empty** / **is not empty**

You can combine multiple conditions with AND/OR logic.

#### Actions (the "THEN" part)

When conditions are met, Tallyfy fires one or more actions on a target step:

- **Visibility** - show or hide a step
- **Assignment** - add assignees, replace all of them, or clear them all from a task
- **Deadline** - set a deadline before or after a starting point you choose, in minutes, hours, days, weeks, or months
- **Status** - reopen a completed task (useful for approval loops)
- **Webhook** - send an HTTP request to an external URL when conditions are met. You can only add this one through the API today; in the editor, use a step's own **Emit a webhook when this task is completed?** setting instead

:::note
Visibility and deadline actions can only target each step once within a single automation.
:::

### When does all this actually run?

An automation runs **at most once per process**. Tallyfy checks automations when the process
launches and when a task is completed or reopened. Nothing watches a form field and reacts when
its value changes.

That one sentence explains most of the surprises people hit, so it has its own page: [when an
automation runs](/products/pro/documenting/templates/automations/when-automations-run/).

### Naming your automations

Good names save time when debugging. Be specific:

- "Show steps for USA customers only" beats "Customer automation"
- "Assign to manager if urgent" beats "Automation 1"

:::tip[Check your automations before anyone runs the process]
A step can end up hidden on every path, or a deadline can count from a step that never happens.
If two automations act on the same step, for example one shows it and one hides it, the result
depends on which one Tallyfy applies last.

Connected AI can walk every path through your template and tell you what is broken, without
launching anything or involving anyone. Just ask: **"Test my template and tell me what is
broken."** See [Test a template before anyone runs
it](/products/pro/integrations/mcp-server/template-testing/).
:::
