## What the widget does

You're chatting with Claude about next quarter's hiring plan, and Claude replies, "I'll create a task for you to review the candidate pipeline on Friday." Without the Task Intent Widget, that's where it ends. You'd switch to Tallyfy in another browser tab, click "new task," fill in a form, copy details over.

With the Task Intent Widget, a small box appears at the bottom of Claude's chat bubble. It shows a one-line summary of the proposed task and a **Create task** button. Click it, check the prefilled details, and click **Create task** again. The task is created in your Tallyfy account, and the widget shows a link to it.

![Task Intent Widget showing inline at the bottom of an AI chat bubble](https://screenshots.tallyfy.com/desktop-ai/task-intent-widget.png)

The widget reads the AI's replies, not what you type. The AI suggests work, you confirm it, and Tallyfy tracks it.

## Three kinds of intent

The widget recognizes three kinds of intent and shows a different prefill for each:

| Intent | When it fires | What the widget creates |
|---|---|---|
| **Create task** | The AI's reply proposes a single piece of work ("I'll create a task to...", "let me add a todo for...", "remember to...") | A new task in Tallyfy with title, optional assignee, optional deadline, optional priority |
| **Launch process** | The AI's reply proposes starting a workflow ("let's launch the customer onboarding process", "run the audit template for Q3", "kick off the review workflow") | A new process from an existing template, with the kickoff form pre-filled if the AI provided enough detail |
| **Edit template** | The AI's reply proposes changing a workflow ("add a review step to the onboarding template", "let's update the onboarding template to include training") | A template edit (add step, remove step, or edit step), shown in the widget for you to confirm |

## Widget states

The widget moves through these states as you use it:

1. **Collapsed** - the default state. Shows a one-line summary of up to 80 characters that starts with the intent, such as "Create task:", and a button named for the intent: **Create task**, **Launch process**, or **Edit template**. About 40 pixels tall, so it doesn't dominate the chat bubble.

2. **Expanded** - click the button on the collapsed widget. Shows the prefilled fields so you can check what the AI suggested, with the same button to confirm and a **Full form** button.

3. **Modal** - click **Full form** to see the same fields in a larger window, where you can confirm or cancel. In v2.0.0 the fields are read-only in both the expanded widget and the modal, so change any detail in Tallyfy after the item is created.

4. **Confirmation** - after Tallyfy confirms the item was created. The widget shows a check mark, "Created in Tallyfy", a **View in Tallyfy** button that opens the item inside the desktop app, and an **Open in browser** button when Tallyfy returns a web link.

5. **Error** - if the request fails. The widget shows the error message and a **Retry** button.

The collapsed, expanded, and error states each have a dismiss (**×**) button.

## How detection works

The widget uses two stages. Stage 1 is a regex check that runs on every AI reply. Stage 2 is an extra AI check, for Claude only.

### Stage 1: regex (always on)

As the AI streams its reply into the chat, a regex pattern matcher checks the reply text each time it grows. It looks for phrases that strongly suggest intent: "create a task", "remind me to", "let's launch", "update the onboarding template", and so on. It skips a match that follows a negation such as "won't" or "don't", and sentences that open with words like "if", "suppose", or "imagine". Stage 1 is cheap, runs locally, and produces a confidence score of 0.7 for any hit.

False positives are acceptable at this stage. If the widget shows up when nobody asked for a task, click **×** to dismiss it. The dismiss is sticky for that chat bubble; the widget won't reappear for the same span of text.

### Stage 2: LLM extraction (Claude only)

For Claude conversations, stage 2 fires after the AI finishes its message (500ms after the last text chunk). It asks Claude to look at the message and extract structured intent into a JSON envelope. This catches intent that regex misses ("we ought to look at..." doesn't match a regex but means "create a task to look at...").

Stage 2 runs only if:

1. The provider supports tool use (only Claude in v2.0.0)
2. Stage 1 found at least one hit in the message, so chit-chat never costs an extra AI call

When stage 1 and stage 2 find the same span of text, stage 2 wins, with a confidence score of 0.95. The widget updates in place; no flicker.

For Codex, Gemini, and Ollama, only stage 1 runs.

## Privacy

Stage 1 runs entirely on your machine. The regex patterns are baked into the desktop app source code; no remote calls.

Stage 2 (Claude only) uses the provider you're already chatting with. It's one extra prompt to Claude per message, which counts against your Claude subscription.

Tallyfy itself sees only the item you confirm: the task, process launch, or template change, with its prefilled fields. The widget doesn't send your prompt text to Tallyfy. The widget doesn't log to a third-party telemetry endpoint.

## What happens after you click Create

The widget sends the item to your Tallyfy account through the desktop app.

On success, the widget switches to the confirmation state. **View in Tallyfy** opens the new item inside the desktop app (`tallyfy://desktop-task/<id>`), and **Open in browser** opens its web link when Tallyfy returns one.

On failure, the widget shows the error message and a **Retry** button. It does *not* collapse back; you already committed to creating something, so the widget stays visible until you either succeed or dismiss.

## Dismissing a widget

There's no setting to turn the widget off in v2.0.0. To hide one, click **×** on it. The dismiss is sticky for that specific bubble. Other widgets in other bubbles still appear.

## Tuning detection accuracy

The regex stage is tested against a corpus of more than 100 example messages, over 30 of them negative examples like sarcasm, hypotheticals, or chit-chat that shouldn't trigger. The tests require precision of at least 0.85 and recall of at least 0.75 on that corpus.

If you find the widget firing too often or too rarely, [tell support](/products/pro/miscellaneous/support/how-can-i-contact-tallyfys-support-team/) the AI's reply and what you expected.

## What it looks like in each provider

The widget looks the same with every provider. What changes is the detection signal:

- **Claude** - both stages run; the highest accuracy.
- **Gemini** - stage 1 only. Detection accuracy is lower than Claude.
- **Codex** - stage 1 only. Codex streams plain text, and the widget watches each line of the reply for patterns.
- **Ollama** - stage 1 only. Works the same as Codex; quality depends on how clearly the local model words its intent.
