## How Spot inspections could feed a Tallyfy procedure

Boston Dynamics makes [Spot](https://bostondynamics.com/products/spot/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/products/spot/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[1]</sup></a>, a robot that walks a site and checks equipment. Other software can [tell Spot to run an inspection](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[2]</sup></a> and read what it found. A written procedure in Tallyfy could take over from there, so a problem the robot flags becomes a task with an owner and a deadline.

Tallyfy doesn't sell that connection. Your team would have to build it.

## What Boston Dynamics makes today

Boston Dynamics has [three robots in its portfolio](https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-appoints-rohit-prasad-as-chief-executive-officer/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-appoints-rohit-prasad-as-chief-executive-officer/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[3]</sup></a>, and software called Orbit.

- **Spot** carries out industrial inspections. [Over 1,500 are in customer hands](https://bostondynamics.com/products/spot/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/products/spot/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[4]</sup></a>, with [customers in more than 40 countries](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/securing-the-fifa-world-cup-2026/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/blog/securing-the-fifa-world-cup-2026/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[5]</sup></a>.
- **Stretch** moves boxes, [up to 50 pounds each](https://bostondynamics.com/products/stretch/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/products/stretch/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[6]</sup></a>.
- **Atlas** is an electric humanoid, ["currently in development"](https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-appoints-rohit-prasad-as-chief-executive-officer/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-appoints-rohit-prasad-as-chief-executive-officer/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[7]</sup></a> as of October 2026.
- **Orbit** [collects and stores the data from every Spot mission](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[8]</sup></a>, and schedules the missions.

Boston Dynamics' [review of Spot's customers](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/retrospective-on-boston-dynamics-spot-robot-uses/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/blog/retrospective-on-boston-dynamics-spot-robot-uses/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[9]</sup></a> counts more than 1 million automated data captures in 2023. It names many customers, among them these four. National Grid piloted Spot at a high-voltage site where no people can go during operation. AB InBev used Spot at a brewery to cut average repair times from a few months to 13 days. UKAEA sent Spot into Chernobyl with a radiation detection payload. In 2023, Purina used Spot's data for predictive maintenance.

## What outside software can ask Spot to do

Through Orbit's API[^1], a program can do the following, as of October 2026:

- [Send Spot on a mission](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/python/bosdyn-orbit/src/bosdyn/orbit/client)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/python/bosdyn-orbit/src/bosdyn/orbit/client" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[10]</sup></a> right away.
- [Schedule a mission](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[11]</sup></a>, which Orbit then starts and monitors.
- Read [each run's status](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/orbit_api)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/orbit_api" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[12]</sup></a>, what each action produced, and any anomalies.
- Get a webhook (a message Orbit pushes to another system) each time [a robot completes an action](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[13]</sup></a>. A separate kind fires when the action raises an alert.
- Receive a work order when an alert occurs. The [Orbit product page](https://bostondynamics.com/products/orbit/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/products/orbit/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[14]</sup></a> labels this beta.

Since release 5.1, Spot can also [pause, redirect or end a mission based on what it sees](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/a-new-perspective-for-facilities-inspection/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/blog/a-new-perspective-for-facilities-inspection/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[15]</sup></a>, with no operator needed.

## What outside software can't do yet

All four limits are as of October 2026.

- None of Orbit's [documented webhooks](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[16]</sup></a> fires when a whole mission ends. They fire once per action, so a program has to ask Orbit for the run's status.
- The parts you'd use most can break. Runs, schedules, webhooks and work orders sit in a tier Boston Dynamics calls ["experimental/beta"](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/orbit_api)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/orbit_api" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[17]</sup></a>, and it may make breaking changes there in any release.
- We found no public developer docs for Stretch or Atlas. The [developer site](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[18]</sup></a> covers Spot and Orbit.
- No MCP interface is documented yet. Boston Dynamics says release 5.2 ["lays the foundation for a new MCP layer"](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/the-missing-piece-of-your-ai-stack/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/blog/the-missing-piece-of-your-ai-stack/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[19]</sup></a>[^2] that would let AI agents deploy Spot. We found no MCP documentation on its developer site.

## Where a written procedure in Tallyfy fits

Spot can find a problem. People still have to fix it. A Tallyfy [template](/products/pro/documenting/templates/) is the written procedure for that fix: the steps, who does each one, and by when.

As a concept, Orbit's alert would go to a small connector your team writes. The connector would call Tallyfy's API to [launch the matching process](/products/pro/launching/triggers/via-api/) and fill in its kick-off form. Tallyfy would then assign the repair and the sign-off to named people. Once the repair is done, a [Tallyfy webhook](/products/pro/integrations/webhooks/) could tell the connector to send Spot back for a second look.

Boston Dynamics describes a similar pattern itself: an application that [creates a work order in an asset management system](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[20]</sup></a> whenever the robot identifies an anomaly.

```d2
# Concept: a Spot alert becomes a tracked Tallyfy task
direction: down

Spot: Spot runs a check {
  style.fill: "#e1f7e6"
  style.stroke: "#225930"
}

Orbit: Orbit flags an alert {
  style.fill: "#f2faf4"
  style.stroke: "#225930"
}

Connector: Your connector {
  style.fill: "#f2faf4"
  style.stroke: "#225930"
  style.stroke-dash: 3
}

Process: Tallyfy process {
  style.fill: "#f2faf4"
  style.stroke: "#225930"
}

Task: Assigned task {
  shape: circle
  style.fill: "#e1f7e6"
  style.stroke: "#225930"
  style.stroke-width: 2
}

Spot -> Orbit: Sends reading
Orbit -> Connector: Sends webhook
Connector -> Process: Calls Tallyfy API
Process -> Task: Assigns work
```

**What to notice:**

- Orbit keeps running the robot. Tallyfy would only track the follow-up work.
- The dashed box is the one piece your team would build and look after.
- One alert on one action starts the process.

## Technical details

(Skip this unless you're setting up the technical side.)

- Spot's [product page](https://bostondynamics.com/products/spot/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/products/spot/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[21]</sup></a> lists a 90-minute average runtime, a 14 kg payload, a top speed of 1.6 m/s and a working range of -20°C to 55°C. Spot [climbs stairs and crosses rough terrain](https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-launches-commercial-sales-of-spot-robot/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-launches-commercial-sales-of-spot-robot/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[22]</sup></a>. No price is published, as of October 2026. Buyers [contact the sales team](https://bostondynamics.com/spot-sales/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/spot-sales/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[23]</sup></a>.
- An operator [drives Spot around the site](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/autonomy/graphnav_tech_summary)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/autonomy/graphnav_tech_summary" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[24]</sup></a>, and a feature called Autowalk records the route so Spot can repeat it. Operators can update the map later. A mission step can also [call outside software](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/python/examples/remote_mission_service/readme)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/python/examples/remote_mission_service/readme" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[25]</sup></a>.
- The [Orbit API guide](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/orbit_api)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/orbit_api" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[26]</sup></a> describes plain HTTPS with a bearer token that an Orbit admin creates. As of October 2026, the stable tier, v1, covers only a few resources so far, including robots, site walks and site elements. The `bosdyn-orbit` Python package wraps only the experimental tier, v0. Each Orbit instance serves its own API reference.
- The two [documented webhook events](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/orbit/about_orbit" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[27]</sup></a> are `ACTION_COMPLETED` and `ACTION_COMPLETED_WITH_ALERT`. Each request carries an `Orbit-Signature` header.
- As of October 2026, the [Spot SDK](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/)<a href="https://dev.bostondynamics.com/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[28]</sup></a> is at version 5.2.0 and the [C++ SDK](https://github.com/boston-dynamics/spot-cpp-sdk)<a href="https://github.com/boston-dynamics/spot-cpp-sdk" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[29]</sup></a> is a beta release.
- Orbit [checks Spot's images against preset prompts](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/see-your-facility/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/blog/see-your-facility/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[30]</sup></a>, for example to flag spills. Its AIVI-Learning models [run on Google Gemini](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/aivi-learning-now-powered-google-gemini-robotics/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/blog/aivi-learning-now-powered-google-gemini-robotics/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[31]</sup></a>, and data sharing with Boston Dynamics is required. Release 5.2 added [gas sensing, visual vibration analysis and partial discharge detection](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/the-missing-piece-of-your-ai-stack/)<a href="https://bostondynamics.com/blog/the-missing-piece-of-your-ai-stack/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[32]</sup></a>.
- On the Tallyfy side, the connector starts a process with `POST https://go.tallyfy.com/api/organizations/{org_id}/runs`. The [launch process reference](/products/pro/integrations/open-api/code-samples/processes/launch-process/) lists the headers and fields.

[^1]: A way for other software to talk to Orbit directly, sending instructions and reading data with nobody at a screen.
[^2]: MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the shared standard that lets AI assistants connect to outside tools.
