## How a FANUC robot could start a Tallyfy procedure

FANUC makes industrial robots and counts [more than 1 million installed worldwide](https://www.fanucamerica.com/)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[1]</sup></a>. It also [publishes a driver](https://www.fanucamerica.com/solutions/physical-ai/ros-2-driver)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/solutions/physical-ai/ros-2-driver" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[2]</sup></a> that lets other software control a robot. So when a robot stops with an error, your own software could start a written procedure in Tallyfy, with an owner and a deadline for each step.

Tallyfy doesn't sell that connection. Your team or your integrator would have to build it.

## What FANUC makes today

As of October 2026:

- **Cobots.** These are robots made to work alongside people. FANUC's cobots, which include the CRX series, [carry from 3 kg to 50 kg](https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/series/collaborative)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/series/collaborative" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[3]</sup></a>. FANUC said in April 2026 that the 3 kg CRX-3iA was [available for customer order](https://www.fanucamerica.com/press-releases/fanuc-america-debuts-ultra-lightweight-crx-3ia-collaborative-robot-and-new-capabilities-across-the-crx-lineup)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/press-releases/fanuc-america-debuts-ultra-lightweight-crx-3ia-collaborative-robot-and-new-capabilities-across-the-crx-lineup" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[4]</sup></a>.
- **Controllers.** A controller is the box that runs a robot's programs. The CRX-10iA cobot [lists the R-30iB Plus](https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/robot/crx-10ia)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/robot/crx-10ia" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[5]</sup></a> as its controller. FANUC calls the [R-50iA its new controller](https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/controller-series/r-50ia)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/controller-series/r-50ia" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[6]</sup></a>, and says the CRX line [will also be available on it](https://www.fanucamerica.com/press-releases/fanuc-america-debuts-ultra-lightweight-crx-3ia-collaborative-robot-and-new-capabilities-across-the-crx-lineup)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/press-releases/fanuc-america-debuts-ultra-lightweight-crx-3ia-collaborative-robot-and-new-capabilities-across-the-crx-lineup" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[7]</sup></a>.
- **Integrators.** FANUC's [authorized system integrators](https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/series/collaborative)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/series/collaborative" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[8]</sup></a> design, build and support custom robot systems.

## What outside software can ask a robot to do

FANUC's driver is for ROS 2[^1]. FANUC says it's [available now](https://www.fanucamerica.com/solutions/physical-ai/ros-2-driver)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/solutions/physical-ai/ros-2-driver" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[9]</sup></a> for the CRX series and several other models, and the [source code is public](https://github.com/FANUC-CORPORATION/fanuc_driver)<a href="https://github.com/FANUC-CORPORATION/fanuc_driver" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[10]</sup></a>.

As of October 2026, a program using the driver can:

- [Read the robot's status](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[11]</sup></a>, such as whether it's in error, stopped by an emergency stop, or moving.
- Read [up to 5 active error messages](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[12]</sup></a>.
- [Read and set input and output signals](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[13]</sup></a>, along with the number registers a robot program reads.
- [Send motion instructions](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[14]</sup></a>, or call a program that's already on the robot.
- See whether the program built from those instructions is [running, paused or aborted](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[15]</sup></a>.
- [Pass motion control back](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/motion_control_authority.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/motion_control_authority.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[16]</sup></a> to the robot's own programs, then take it again.

FANUC documents three other routes:

- **MQTT** is a standard for passing short messages between machines. With FANUC's MQTT option, the robot [connects to a message broker](https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/software/robot/mqtt)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/software/robot/mqtt" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[17]</sup></a> and publishes, subscribes or both.
- **OPC UA** is a standard for sharing factory data. FANUC's OPC UA Server lets [up to 10 clients](https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/software/robot/hmi-device-snpx)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/products/software/robot/hmi-device-snpx" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[18]</sup></a> on the same network read, write and subscribe to robot information.
- **Python** runs [on the R-50iA controller itself](https://www.fanucamerica.com/solutions/physical-ai/fanuc-python-programming)<a href="https://www.fanucamerica.com/solutions/physical-ai/fanuc-python-programming" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[19]</sup></a>, as a standard feature. FANUC's page names no other controller. A Python program can use the robot's signals and register values. With the optional Remote Motion Interface, a Python program can also move the robot.

## What outside software can't do

All four limits are as of October 2026.

- The driver's documented [topics and services](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[20]</sup></a> include no job or mission call, so your software sends instructions or calls a robot program.
- The driver docs describe no webhooks (messages pushed to another system's web address). It [publishes status on ROS 2 topics](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[21]</sup></a>, and a program has to subscribe to them.
- The driver won't run on every controller. It needs [two controller software options](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/environment/system_requirements.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/environment/system_requirements.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[22]</sup></a>, Stream Motion and Remote Motion, or a package that includes both. If your controller lacks them, the quick start guide says to [contact FANUC to obtain them](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/quick_start/quick_start.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/quick_start/quick_start.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[23]</sup></a>. FANUC's pages list no price for them, so ask.
- The command manual isn't in the driver docs, which [point you to FANUC's Remote Motion Interface manual](https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html)<a href="https://fanuc-corporation.github.io/fanuc_driver_doc/main/docs/fanuc_driver/controller_usage.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><sup>[24]</sup></a>.

## Where a written procedure in Tallyfy fits

A robot can report that it stopped. People still have to find the cause, fix it and sign off. A Tallyfy [template](/products/pro/documenting/templates/) is the written procedure for that work: the steps, who does each one, and by when.

As a concept, a small connector your team writes would watch the robot's status. When the robot reports an error, the connector would call Tallyfy's API to [launch the matching process](/products/pro/launching/triggers/via-api/) and put the error text into its kick-off form. Tallyfy would then assign the repair and the sign-off to named people. Once the sign-off is complete, a [Tallyfy webhook](/products/pro/integrations/webhooks/) could tell the connector to set a signal that the robot's own program waits for.

You don't need a connector to begin. A person can type what the robot shows into a Tallyfy [form field](/products/pro/tracking-and-tasks/tasks/what-are-form-fields-in-tallyfy/).

Keep safety with the robot's controller and your own safety review. Tallyfy would only track the work people do.

- On the Tallyfy side, the connector starts a process with `POST https://go.tallyfy.com/api/organizations/{org_id}/runs`. The [launch process reference](/products/pro/integrations/open-api/code-samples/processes/launch-process/) lists the headers and fields.
