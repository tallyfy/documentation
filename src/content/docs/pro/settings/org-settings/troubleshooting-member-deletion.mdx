## Troubleshooting member deletion

Removing a member takes two steps. First you deactivate them with **Deactivate account** on their profile. Then, if you want them gone for good, you click **Delete** in their row of the **Members** list. The messages below are the ones Tallyfy shows when a step can't go ahead.

### "Cannot modify the default administrator."

The full message reads: *"Cannot modify the default administrator. Please assign another member as default administrator first."*

You'll see it when the member is an Administrator and no other active Administrator exists. Tallyfy won't leave your organization without one. Make another member an Administrator, then deactivate the original member. If the member was the default administrator, Tallyfy passes that role to another active Administrator for you.

It also appears on the **Automatic conflict resolution** page when the member is the default administrator and you hand their work to someone who isn't an Administrator. The person you pick becomes the default administrator, so pick an Administrator.

If they're the only member of your organization, they're also its only Administrator, so they can't be removed. To close the organization instead, contact support.

### "Please remove the user assignments from the templates and steps to proceed."

You clicked **Deactivate member** to skip the handover, and the member has both templates and template steps. Tallyfy won't skip the handover in that case. Pick one person under **Re-assign all NAME's tasks and steps to:** instead, and they'll take over everything.

### "Please disable the user before deletion."

The API was asked to delete a member who is still active. Deactivate them first, then delete them. In the web app, the **Delete** button stays greyed out until the member is deactivated, and hovering over it says "Please deactivate the member first".

### "Bot users cannot be disabled."

Bot accounts (like Tallyfy Bot) can't be removed or disabled. They don't count toward billing - they handle automated comments and system tasks.

When removing a member through the `DELETE /organizations/{org}/users/{user_id}` endpoint, you'll see *"You can't remove a bot user from this organization!"* instead.

### "Someone else is changing this group or one of its tasks right now. Try again in a moment."

Someone else was changing one of the member's groups, or a task one of those groups is on, at the same moment. Tallyfy waited a few seconds for that change to finish, then stopped. The member is still active, and none of their work has moved. If they were the default administrator, though, that role may already have passed to another Administrator. Try the removal again.

### A deactivated member says they can still get in

They can no longer get at your organization through the web app or the API, even with an API token they already have. Tallyfy checks every signed-in request to your organization, and refuses theirs, usually with *"You are not an active member of this organization!"* Links that need no sign-in, such as a public kick-off form or a file download link, still open for anyone who has them, the member included. If they belong to other Tallyfy organizations, they can still use those. If someone really is still working in yours, check that their **Status** in the **Members** list reads **Deactivated**.

### "Form inviter is disabled. Please contact your organization to update."

People see this on a public kick-off form. The form was tied to a member who was deactivated without a handover, and that member also owned the template. Pick a new inviter in the template's kick-off form settings. The steps are in [remove a member](/products/pro/documenting/members/how-can-i-remove-a-member-from-my-tallyfy-organization/).

### Can't find a deactivated member

1. Go to the **Members** list
2. Use the **Status** column's filter and choose **Deactivated**
3. Search by name or email

### What happens to their work

One person takes over everything. On the **Automatic conflict resolution** page you pick one active member. Tallyfy moves the templates the member created, owns or belongs to, and their template step assignments. It also moves the processes they started, own or belong to, the tasks they created, and every task assigned to them. Their [scheduled jobs](/products/pro/launching/triggers/via-recurring-schedule/) move too, if the new person can run them. The new person gets an email. The member is taken out of all their groups, and any access they had to specific templates or processes stays with them.

Work never falls to "Tallyfy Bot". The bot can't be assigned tasks at all (see [member statuses](/products/pro/settings/org-settings/understanding-member-statuses/)). When a deactivation skips the handover, on the page or through the API without `with_reassignment=true`, the work stays with the deactivated member. Nothing moves on its own, so reassign it yourself when you're ready. Their scheduled jobs fail until you change each job's owner, so change the owners before you deactivate them.

### What permanent deletion does

If the person also belongs to another Tallyfy organization, deleting them only removes them from yours. Their account, name, email and other organizations aren't touched.

If they don't belong anywhere else, Tallyfy deletes their account and:
- Appends "(Deleted)" directly to the last name (no space) - e.g. `Smith` becomes `Smith(Deleted)`
- Changes email to `{email}.deleted.{user_id}` - e.g. `john@company.com` becomes `john@company.com.deleted.12345`

The new email lets you invite that person again later. Their completed work and history stay in your organization, under the changed name. There's no way to undo a deletion yourself, so deactivate instead if they might come back.

### Removing members through the API

Deactivating and then deleting takes two calls for each member:
```
1. DELETE /organizations/{org}/users/{user_id}/disable
2. Wait for 200 response
3. DELETE /organizations/{org}/users/{user_id}/delete
```

A delete sent before the disable is refused with "Please disable the user before deletion." A call that answers `409` with the group message above didn't remove the member or move their work, so send it again. As above, the default administrator role may already have moved.

To hand their work to someone as you deactivate them, use this call instead of the disable call:
```
DELETE /organizations/{org}/users/{user_id}?with_reassignment=true&to={new_user_id}
```

It deactivates the member and hands their templates, processes and tasks to the person in `to`, along with the scheduled jobs that person can run. `to` must be an active member of your organization, not the organization's bot, and not the member you're removing. Otherwise the call answers `422` and nothing changes. The call doesn't delete the member. To delete them permanently, call `DELETE /organizations/{org}/users/{user_id}/delete` afterwards.

### Before you remove someone

- Keep at least two active Administrators, so removing one never stops on the default administrator error.
- Change the owner of each of their scheduled jobs.
- Check whether they're the inviter on any public kick-off form.
- Contact support if you see a message that isn't on this page, a server error (500), or you deleted someone by mistake. Send your organization name, the member's email, the exact message and a screenshot.
